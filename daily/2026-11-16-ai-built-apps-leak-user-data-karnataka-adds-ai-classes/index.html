Daily AI Update · 16 November 2026

AI-Built Apps Leak User Data, Karnataka Adds AI Classes and More

5 min read · 16 November 2026

Good morning! Today we start with an important warning for anyone building apps with AI tools, then look at AI classes in Karnataka schools, a new AI deal India signed, and what WhatsApp's new boss plans next.

1. Thousands of apps made with AI builder Lovable left user data open

Lovable is a popular tool that builds websites and apps from a simple text prompt. German security firm Crissy Field checked 159,041 Lovable projects. Of the 34,545 that used a Supabase database, 24,664 showed their data to anyone, with no login needed. Exposed data included email addresses, over 200,000 private chat messages, more than 1,000 passport records, and restaurant and event orders. Lovable itself was not hacked. The problem was missing access rules (called Row Level Security) in each app's database. Lovable called it a known, industry-wide setup issue.

Why it matters for you: If you or your business built a booking form, shop or app with an AI builder, ask an expert to check who can read your database before you collect customer details. 'Easy to build' does not mean 'safe by default'.

Source: Cybernews

2. Karnataka brings AI lessons to Class 9 in 1,800 government schools

Karnataka's school education department is adding an AI subject for Class 9 students in 1,800 government high schools from the 2026-27 year. It is a skill (vocational) subject under the national NSQF framework. Students will learn AI basics, Python, working with data, and simple ideas behind computer vision, language AI and generative AI, with maths topics like statistics and probability. Each school will hire one AI guest teacher on Rs 20,000 a month. Students can take it in place of a third language. Officials say AI learning may later extend up to Class 12.

Why it matters for you: Government school students will get early, hands-on AI and coding practice. Graduates with a computer science or IT degree and one year of work experience can look out for these guest teacher posts.

Source: Elets digitalLEARNING

3. India signs new US-led pact to protect AI supply chains

On November 15 in San Francisco, the United States and eight partners, including India, Japan, Australia, the UK and South Korea, signed a new declaration under the 'Pax Silica' plan. India first joined Pax Silica in February at the AI Impact Summit in Delhi. The new step says countries will list the AI technology and infrastructure that most needs protection, work together to keep supplies of chips, minerals and energy safe, and build shared AI industry and investment hubs. Working groups will handle the details. The main aim is to depend less on China.

Why it matters for you: This can bring more chip, data centre and electronics work to India over time. For UPSC and other exam aspirants, Pax Silica is a current affairs topic worth noting.

Source: Poland Insight

4. WhatsApp's new CEO Kunal Shah shares plans for AI and payments

Kunal Shah, the CRED founder who became WhatsApp's global CEO in June, posted his three big plans for the app. First, richer chats. Second, more services inside WhatsApp, like bill payments, loans and eSIMs, with banking and telecom partners. Third, using AI to handle bookings and appointments for users. He did not share partner names or dates. WhatsApp's bill payment feature already launched in India in September, and Meta's Muse AI agent is still on a waitlist for Indian users.

Why it matters for you: Small businesses on WhatsApp should get ready for customers booking appointments through AI in chat. Keep your WhatsApp Business catalogue, timings and prices up to date so AI can find them.

Source: MediaNama
