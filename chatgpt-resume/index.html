How to Write a Resume with ChatGPT: Step-by-Step Guide with Prompts

8 min read · Updated October 2026

Writing a resume is hard when you're a fresher. What do you write when you have no work experience? How do you make a college project sound impressive without lying?

AI chatbots like ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude are very good at this, as long as you give them the right information and check what they write. This guide walks you through it step by step, with prompts you can copy.

Before you start: what AI can and can't do

  • AI is great at turning rough notes into clear bullet points, fixing grammar, matching your resume to a job description and suggesting stronger action words.
  • AI can't know what you actually did. If you give it vague input, it fills the gaps with made-up numbers and skills. Never put anything on your resume that you can't explain in an interview.
  • Protect your privacy. Don't paste your Aadhaar, PAN, full address or phone number into a chatbot. Add those yourself at the end.

Step 1: Collect your raw material

Spend 10 minutes writing rough notes, in any order and any language. Include:

  • Degree, college, year and CGPA or percentage
  • Projects: what you built, the tools you used, and any result (users, accuracy, marks)
  • Internships, part-time work, freelancing or family business help
  • Certifications and online courses
  • Technical and soft skills you can actually demonstrate
  • Achievements: hackathons, ranks, college events you organised

Step 2: Turn your notes into resume bullets

PromptYou are an experienced recruiter in India. Turn my rough notes into resume bullet points. Start each bullet with a strong action verb, keep each under 20 words, and include numbers only where I've given them. Do not invent any facts, numbers or skills. If something important is missing, ask me instead of guessing. My notes: [paste your notes]

The line "Do not invent any facts" is the most important part. Without it, AI often adds impressive-sounding numbers that aren't true.

Step 3: Write a short summary

PromptWrite a 2-line professional summary for my resume. I'm a [degree] [year/fresher] applying for [role] jobs in [city/remote]. My strongest points are [2–3 points]. Avoid clichés like "hardworking" and "team player".

Step 4: Match your resume to each job

Many companies use software (an ATS) that filters resumes by keywords before a human sees them. Tailoring your resume to each job description makes a big difference.

PromptHere is a job description and my resume. List the important skills and keywords from the job description that are missing from my resume. Then suggest where I could honestly add them, based only on what's already in my resume. Job description: [paste] My resume: [paste]

Step 5: Get honest feedback

PromptReview my resume like a strict HR manager hiring for [role]. Give me the 5 biggest problems, in order of importance, and how to fix each one. Be direct. [paste resume]

Step 6: Fit it on one page

For freshers and anyone with less than about 5 years of experience, one page is the standard. If your resume runs to two or three pages, paste it into the free one-page resume maker. It keeps your strongest points, drops filler like hobbies and declarations, and lets you edit and download a clean A4 PDF. It runs in your browser, so your resume isn't uploaded anywhere.

Before and after

✗ Weak: "Did a project on machine learning."

✓ Strong: "Built a Python model to predict crop prices from 5 years of mandi data, reaching 87% accuracy, as a final-year project."

Only use details like these if they're true for your project. The point is to be specific about what you actually did.

Fresher resume checklist

  • Name, email, phone, city and LinkedIn or GitHub at the top
  • 2-line summary aimed at the role
  • Education with CGPA or percentage
  • 2–3 projects with tools and results
  • Internships or work experience, if any
  • Skills grouped (for example: Languages, Tools, Soft skills)
  • Certifications and achievements
  • No photo, date of birth, father's name or declaration unless the employer asks for them
  • Saved as PDF with a clear file name, like Priya-Sharma-Resume.pdf

Ready-to-copy prompts for job hunting

Cover letterWrite a 150-word cover letter for the [role] position at [company], based on my resume below. Mention why I'm interested in this company specifically: [reason]. Keep it warm and professional. [paste resume]
LinkedIn headlineSuggest 5 LinkedIn headlines under 120 characters for a [degree] fresher looking for [role] jobs, based on these skills: [skills].
Interview practiceAct as an interviewer for a [role] fresher job at an Indian company. Ask me one question at a time based on my resume, wait for my answer, and give short feedback before the next question. [paste resume]

Frequently asked questions

Can recruiters tell if a resume was written by ChatGPT?
Generic phrases like "results-driven professional" and "passionate team player" are a giveaway. Specific details about your own projects aren't. Use AI to polish your real experience, then edit the final text in your own words.

Is it okay to use AI for my resume?
Yes, as long as everything on it is true. AI is a writing assistant, like a friend checking your draft.

Which AI is best for writing a resume?
ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude all do this well on their free plans. The quality depends far more on the notes you give it than on which tool you use.

Conclusion

Give AI real, detailed notes, tell it not to invent anything, tailor your resume to each job, and keep it to one page. Then read every line and ask yourself: "Can I explain this in an interview?"
