How to Prepare for AKTU Semester Exams Using Previous Year Papers and AI

7 min read · Updated October 2026

Ask any AKTU senior how they passed a tough subject in the last two weeks and you'll hear the same answer: "Solve the previous year papers." AKTU repeats topics, and sometimes whole questions, across years.

This guide shows you a simple plan to use previous year question papers (PYQs) properly, and how to use free AI tools to understand answers instead of just memorising them.

Understand the AKTU paper pattern first

Recent AKTU B.Tech theory papers (for example Machine Learning Techniques 2025-26) follow this pattern: 3 hours, 70 marks, three sections.

SectionWhat you doMarks
AAnswer all 7 short questions (2 marks each)14
BAnswer any 3 of 5 long questions (7 marks each)21
CFive questions (Q3–Q7), one per unit; answer one of two parts in each (7 marks each)35

Section C is the key insight: every unit gets its own question, so you can't skip a whole unit. But you get a choice within each unit, so you don't need to know every topic either.

Patterns can differ by subject code and year. Check the latest paper for your subject on our question papers page.

The 5-step PYQ plan

Step 1: Collect 4–5 years of papers

Find your subject on the AKTU question papers page. We have 180+ papers for AI, ML, data science, Python and related subjects, with every question typed out as text, so you can search them and paste them into AI tools easily. Note that subject codes change between syllabus versions (for example KCS055 became BCS055), so look at related codes too.

Step 2: Find the repeated topics

Copy the questions from all your papers into one document, then ask AI to sort them:

PromptHere are questions from 5 years of AKTU [subject] exam papers. Group them by topic, count how many years each topic appeared, and list topics from most to least repeated. Mark which ones were 2-mark and which were 7-mark questions. [paste questions]

Always spot-check the counts against the papers. AI is good at grouping but sometimes miscounts.

Step 3: Learn the top topics unit by unit

Start with topics that appeared 3 or more times. For each one:

PromptExplain [topic] from AKTU [subject] in simple words for a B.Tech student. Then write a model answer for this 7-mark question in about 400 words, with headings, a labelled diagram description and one example: [paste question]

Read the explanation, then check the model answer against your class notes or textbook. Your syllabus and teacher's notes are the final authority on what to write.

Step 4: Write answers yourself, then get them checked

Reading answers isn't enough. Close the book, write the answer by hand with a timer (about 12–15 minutes for a 7-mark answer), then type or photograph it and ask:

PromptCheck my answer to this AKTU 7-mark question like a strict examiner. Give a mark out of 7, list what's missing, and tell me what would get full marks. Question: [question] My answer: [answer]

Step 5: Do one full mock paper

2–3 days before the exam, take the latest paper, set a 3-hour timer and write the whole thing. This shows you if your time management works, which is where many students lose marks.

A 7-day plan for one subject

DayTask
1Collect papers, list repeated topics
2Units 1 and 2: top topics + 2-mark definitions
3Units 3 and 4: top topics
4Unit 5 + practise drawing important diagrams
5Write 5 long answers with a timer and get them checked
6Full 3-hour mock with the latest paper
7Revise weak topics and all 2-mark answers

Tips for writing answers that score

  • Section A: a definition plus one example or one line of explanation is usually enough. Don't write a page for 2 marks.
  • Long answers: use headings, a diagram where it fits, and an example. Examiners skim, so make your answer easy to scan.
  • Algorithms: write the steps as a numbered list, then a small worked example.
  • Don't leave anything blank. A partial answer can still earn marks.

Using AI the right way for exam prep

  • Do use it to explain topics, generate practice questions and check your written answers.
  • Don't trust it blindly. It can get formulas, derivations and definitions slightly wrong. Verify with your textbook.
  • Try free tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or NotebookLM. See our list of free AI tools for students.

Frequently asked questions

Do AKTU questions repeat every year?
Topics repeat often, and some questions come back with small changes in wording. That's why solving 4–5 years of papers is one of the best ways to prepare. It doesn't replace studying the syllabus.

How many years of previous papers should I solve?
4–5 years is a good balance. If your subject code changed recently, include papers from the older code too, since the syllabus is usually similar.

Where can I find AKTU previous year papers for free?
On the LearnAIFast question papers page, with every question as text and the original PDF linked.

Conclusion

Collect 4–5 years of papers, find the repeated topics, learn them unit by unit, write answers yourself and get them checked, and finish with a timed mock. Start now: find your subject's papers.
